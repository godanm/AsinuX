How to Play

🏆 Objective

Beat the dealer by getting a hand value closer to 21 without going over. You play against the house — not other players. It's a simple concept with rich decision-making beneath the surface.

  • Get closer to 21 than the dealer
  • Go over 21 and you "bust" — instant loss
  • Dealer busts? You win automatically
  • Equal totals = push (bet returned, no chips won or lost)

🃏 Card Values

Number cards are worth their face value. Face cards are all worth 10. Aces are uniquely flexible — they can count as 1 or 11, whichever benefits your hand more.

  • 2 through 10 = face value
  • Jack, Queen, King = 10 each
  • Ace = 11, but counts as 1 if 11 would cause a bust
  • Ace + any 10-value card = Blackjack (the best hand — pays 1.5×)

🎯 Your Actions

After the initial deal (two cards each, one dealer card hidden), you choose how to play your hand. Pick wisely — the dealer's visible card is your key clue.

  • Hit — draw another card to increase your total
  • Stand — keep your current hand and end your turn
  • Double Down — double your bet, draw exactly one more card, then stand automatically. Only available on your first two cards.

🤖 Dealer Rules

The dealer follows strict, fixed rules — there's no decision-making or strategy on their side. One of the dealer's two cards stays hidden until after your turn ends.

  • Dealer always hits until reaching 17 or higher
  • Dealer stands on 17, 18, 19, 20, and 21
  • The hidden card is revealed after you Stand or Bust
  • If the dealer busts, all players still in the hand win

💰 Payouts

Your winnings depend on how you won. Blackjack (natural 21 on the first two cards) pays a special bonus above a regular win.

  • Regular win → +1× your bet
  • Blackjack (Ace + 10-value card) → +1.5× your bet
  • Push (tie) → bet returned, no gain or loss
  • Bust or lower total → lose your bet

Basic Strategy Guide

🧠 The Fundamentals of Smart Play

Blackjack has a mathematically proven "basic strategy" — optimal decisions for every possible combination of your hand and the dealer's visible card. You don't need to memorise it all, but these key principles will significantly improve your results:

  • Always stand on 17 or higher. The risk of busting outweighs any potential gain from hitting. Even a soft 17 (Ace + 6) is best left alone in most situations.
  • Always hit on 8 or lower. You can't bust — any card drawn keeps you in the game. Take the card.
  • Stand on 12–16 when dealer shows 2–6. The dealer is in the "bust zone" — they must hit and are likely to go over 21. Don't take extra risk; let the dealer bust.
  • Hit on 12–16 when dealer shows 7 or higher. The dealer has a strong visible card and is unlikely to bust. You need a better total to compete.
  • Double Down on 10 or 11. When your first two cards total 10 or 11, you have a great chance of hitting 20 or 21 with one more card. Double your bet and take one card.
  • Double Down on 9 vs dealer's 3–6. The dealer is weak; doubling down here gives you a statistical edge.
  • Never chase a bust loss. Each hand is independent. Playing recklessly to recover losses is the fastest way to drain your chip stack.

History & Origins

📖 About Blackjack

Blackjack is one of the most recognised card games in the world, with origins in 18th-century France where it was known as "Vingt-et-Un" (twenty-one). The game crossed the Atlantic with French colonists and became extremely popular in early American casinos, where a special bonus was offered for a hand containing the Ace of Spades and a black Jack — giving the game its modern name.

Over the 20th century, mathematicians developed the first complete basic strategy for Blackjack, published in 1956 by Roger Baldwin and colleagues. This was a landmark moment — proving for the first time that a card game could be played with near-optimal decisions based purely on mathematics. Later work by Edward O. Thorp popularised card counting, permanently changing how casinos approach the game.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a "soft" hand vs a "hard" hand?

A "soft" hand contains an Ace that is currently counted as 11 (e.g., Ace + 6 = soft 17). A "hard" hand has no Ace, or an Ace that must count as 1 to avoid busting (e.g., Ace + 6 + 10 = hard 17). Soft hands are safer to hit because the Ace can drop to 1 if you draw a high card.

When should I Double Down?

Double Down is strongest when your two cards total 10 or 11 — you have a high probability of drawing a 10-value card and ending up with 20 or 21. Also consider doubling on 9 if the dealer shows a 3, 4, 5, or 6 (the "bust zone").

What happens if both I and the dealer get Blackjack?

It's a push — your bet is returned and no chips are won or lost. Both having natural Blackjack on the same hand results in a tie.

Can the dealer get Blackjack?

Yes. If the dealer's hidden card combines with their visible card to make an Ace + 10-value hand, they have Blackjack. This beats any non-Blackjack player hand, including totals of 21 built with 3+ cards.

Does Tricksy use a single deck or multiple decks?
