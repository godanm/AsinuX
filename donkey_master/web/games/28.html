How to Play

🏆 Objective

Win enough game points to reach the target before the opposing team does. Teams bid on how many card points they'll win each round and must at least meet their bid to score.

  • 4 players in 2 teams (A & B)
  • Seats 0 & 2 form Team A; seats 1 & 3 form Team B
  • First team to reach the target wins the game
  • One round = one full set of 8 tricks

🃏 Cards & Points

Game 28 uses only 8 ranks from a standard deck — 32 cards total for 4 players getting 8 each. Card points are counted when you win a trick.

  • Jack = 3 points (highest value)
  • Nine = 2 points
  • Ace = 1 point, Ten = 1 point
  • King, Queen, 8, 7 = 0 points
  • Total card points in the deck = 28 (hence the name)
  • Card rank order (high to low): J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7

🎯 Bidding

Each player gets 4 cards to bid on. Players bid how many card points their team will win this round. The highest bidder wins the bid and secretly chooses a trump suit. The remaining 4 cards are then dealt out.

  • Minimum bid is 14 (half the total points)
  • Maximum bid is 28 — declaring Thani
  • The first bidder must open — cannot pass
  • Partners must bid 20 or higher to outbid each other
  • Bid winner secretly names a trump suit after receiving all 8 cards
  • Bidding 28 = Thani — highest stakes, instant bid win

🔄 Playing Tricks

Players must follow the lead suit if they have it. The highest card of the lead suit wins the trick — unless trump is played. The bid-winning team cannot voluntarily lead trump until it has been revealed.

  • Must follow the lead suit if you hold one
  • Trump beats any non-trump card
  • To reveal trump: be void in the lead suit, then request trump from your partner
  • Once trump is revealed, it applies for the rest of the round
  • Highest trump wins any trick containing trump cards

📊 Scoring

After all 8 tricks, count the card points each team won. Compare the bid team's total against their bid to award game points for the round.

  • Bid met (bid < 20): +1 game point to bid team
  • Bid met (bid ≥ 20): +2 game points to bid team
  • All 28 points won (Thani success): +3 game points to bid team
  • Thani declared but failed: opposing team gets +3 game points
  • Bid failed (any non-Thani): defending team gets the game points instead
  • First team to reach the target score wins the game!

Strategy & Tips

🧠 Winning at Game 28

Game 28 rewards teams that bid accurately and manage trump wisely. Overbidding is costly; underbidding is a missed opportunity. Here's how to sharpen your game:

  • Bid based on your Jacks and Nines. These two cards account for 5 of the 28 points. If your first 4 cards include both Jacks, you can be very aggressive. One Jack = bid around 14–16; two Jacks = bid 18–22+.
  • Choose trump wisely. Pick the suit where you have the most high cards — particularly the Jack of that suit. If you hold the Jack of Spades, making Spades trump guarantees you at least 3 points from that card alone.
  • Communicate with your partner. In Game 28, you cannot talk openly, but the timing of your trump reveal signals your strength to your partner. Revealing trump early shows confidence; delaying it is often a tell of a weaker hand.
  • Win tricks early with high cards. Don't hoard your Jack — play it to win a valuable trick rather than waste it on a zero-point trick at the end.
  • Defend by forcing the bid team to reveal trump. Lead with suits the bid winner is likely void in, forcing them to reveal trump before they're ready.
  • Thani is high risk, high reward. Only declare Thani (bid 28) if your combined hand with partner is exceptionally strong — both Jacks, both Nines, and strong trump coverage. Failing Thani hands 3 points to your opponents instantly.

History & Origins

📖 About Game 28

Game 28 is one of South India's most cherished card games, with deep roots in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. The "28" refers to the total card points in the deck — the sum of all Jacks (3 pts × 4 = 12), Nines (2 pts × 4 = 8), Aces (1 pt × 4 = 4), and Tens (1 pt × 4 = 4). The game is sometimes called "Twenty-Eight" or simply "28" across its home regions.

The game is part of the same family as classic European trick-taking games like Jass and Belote, adapted over generations for South Indian sensibilities. It is particularly beloved in Kerala, where it is played across generations — from village courtyards to metropolitan living rooms. The bidding mechanic, secret trump selection, and Thani declaration make it one of the most strategically rich card games in the region.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can the bid winner not lead trump immediately?

The rule prevents the bid-winning team from using their trump advantage too early and dominating the round unfairly. Trump must be revealed organically — either when someone is void in the lead suit and requests it, or when the bid winner leads a suit that forces a trump reveal from their partner.

What does "partner rule" mean in bidding?

Partners must bid at least 20 to outbid each other. This prevents a team from running up the bid internally while the other team has no chance to compete. It ensures the bid reflects genuine hand strength.

Can I see all 8 cards before choosing trump?

Yes. The bid winner receives all 8 cards before secretly naming the trump suit. This is important — you base your trump choice on your full hand, not just your bidding hand.

What if a team wins exactly their bid?

That counts as a successful bid. Bid met = game points awarded. The bid team doesn't need to win more than their bid — matching it exactly is enough.

What happens if nobody bids above 14?

The minimum bid is 14 — the first player must always open with at least 14. Bidding continues until all other players pass, and the highest bid wins. Ties are broken by seat position.

Is Game 28 the same as 29?

Related but different. Game 29 is a variant popular in North India and Bangladesh that uses a different card set and slightly different bidding rules. Game 28 is the South Indian variant with the 32-card deck (J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7) and the 28-point total.
