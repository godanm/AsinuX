🃏 Donkey (Kazhutha)

Trick-taking · 4 players · Escape before the Donkey tag finds you

How to Play

🏃 Objective

Empty your hand before everyone else. The last player still holding cards is eliminated as the Donkey. Survivors advance through rounds until one champion remains.

  • 4 players, 13 cards each
  • Survive rounds to win the tournament
  • Last player standing wins overall

🃏 Setup

Cards are dealt evenly from a standard 52-card deck. The player holding the Ace of Spades leads the very first trick of the game.

  • Standard deck, no jokers
  • Ace of Spades always leads round 1
  • Cards are sorted by suit in your hand

🔄 Playing a Trick

The leader plays any card — that suit becomes the lead suit. Everyone else must follow the same suit if they have one. The highest card of the lead suit wins the trick, and the winner leads next.

  • Must follow the lead suit if you have it
  • Highest lead-suit card wins the trick
  • Winner leads the next trick
  • There are no trumps — only the lead suit matters

✂️ The Cut

If you have no cards of the lead suit, play any card from your hand. This is called a "cut." The trick ends immediately and all played cards go to the player holding the highest card of the lead suit.

  • Cuts force the highest lead-suit card holder to pick up all cards
  • That player then leads the next trick
  • Play low useless cards when cutting — save good cards
  • Multiple players can cut the same trick

🏆 Escaping & Scoring

Play your last card to escape the round. You're out of that round — safe until the next. The very last player still holding cards becomes the Donkey and is eliminated.

  • 1st to escape: +100 pts
  • 2nd to escape: +60 pts
  • 3rd to escape: +30 pts
  • Donkey (last player): −50 pts, eliminated from tournament

Strategy & Tips

🧠 Winning Strategy

Donkey is not about winning tricks — it's about offloading your hand while forcing high cards onto opponents. Understanding the cut mechanic is the key to winning consistently.

  • Fear Aces and Kings. High cards guarantee you win tricks and then must lead again, giving you more cards to collect. Shed them through a cut when possible.
  • Master the cut. When void in the lead suit, play the most useless card in your hand — preferably a high card in a suit opponents are void in too.
  • Watch hand sizes. A player down to 2–3 cards will play aggressively. Time your cuts to dump cards before they escape.
  • Lead low, lead often. Low cards (2–5) are safer leads — they're less likely to win, meaning someone else picks up the trick.
  • Save your small cards as cuts. Keep at least one small card in every suit so you can cut when needed without wasting a good card.
  • Count suits carefully. Track which suits have been heavily played — once a suit is almost exhausted, cutting in that suit becomes more likely for everyone.

History & Origins

📖 About the Game

Donkey — known as "Kazhutha" (கழுதை / കഴുത, literally "donkey" in Tamil and Malayalam) — is one of the most beloved social card games across Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and South India. It belongs to the family of trick-avoidance games, where the goal is to unload your hand rather than capture tricks — a sharp contrast to games like Bridge or Whist.

The game has been passed down through generations, played enthusiastically during family gatherings, festivals, and long train journeys. Its simplicity makes it accessible to all ages, while the cut mechanic adds enough depth to reward strategic play. The loser's "Donkey" label is delivered with great good-natured teasing, making it as much a social event as a card game.

On Tricksy, the classic Kazhutha rules are preserved exactly — 4 players, 13 cards each, no trumps, and the brutal elimination format that keeps every round tense until the last card is played.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when two players both cut on the same trick?

The player holding the highest card of the lead suit still collects all played cards — including both cut cards. Both cutters benefit from getting rid of unwanted cards, while one unlucky player with a high lead-suit card pays the price.

Can I choose any card when I cut?

Yes. When you're void in the lead suit you may play any card in your hand. This is your chance to dump high cards you don't want — but be thoughtful, as cut cards still end up with the trick winner.

Is there a trump suit?

No. Unlike many trick-taking games, Donkey has no trump suit. Only the lead suit matters. The highest card of the lead suit always wins — cuts simply trigger a forced pickup rather than winning the trick.

What if all players are void in the lead suit?

If every remaining player cuts, the trick still goes to whoever held the highest lead-suit card at the start of the trick — the original leader. They collect all the cut cards.

Does the Ace of Spades always lead?

Only at the very start of round 1. The player holding the Ace of Spades must lead it first. After that, the winner of each trick leads the next — the Ace of Spades has no special role once played.

What happens after someone becomes the Donkey?

The Donkey player receives −50 points and is eliminated from the tournament. The remaining 3 players start a new round until only one champion is left standing.
