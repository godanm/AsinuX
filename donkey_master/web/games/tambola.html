How to Play

🏆 Objective

Each player gets a 3×9 ticket containing 15 numbers. Numbers 1–90 are drawn automatically by the caller. Mark your numbers as they are called. Claim prizes when your pattern is complete before anyone else.

  • Columns 1–9 map to number ranges: col 1 = 1–9, col 2 = 10–19, ..., col 9 = 80–90
  • Each row contains exactly 5 numbers and 4 blank spaces
  • Five separate prizes are available — claim each one as you complete it
  • Full House ends the game for all players

🎫 Your Ticket

Your Tambola ticket is a 3-row by 9-column grid. Each row has exactly 5 numbers and 4 blank spaces — 15 numbers in total across the full ticket.

  • Numbers are auto-marked in orange as they are called
  • Left column (col 1) contains numbers from 1–9
  • Second column contains numbers from 10–19
  • Each subsequent column covers the next ten numbers
  • Right column (col 9) contains numbers from 80–90
  • No number appears more than once on any ticket

🥇 The Five Prizes

Tambola has five distinct prizes. Each prize can only be claimed once — the first player to complete the pattern wins it. Tap the CLAIM button the moment it appears on screen.

  • Early Five — the first five numbers on your ticket to be called. This is the quickest prize to win and the most hotly contested in the opening numbers.
  • Top Line — all five numbers in your ticket's first (top) row are called.
  • Middle Line — all five numbers in your ticket's second (middle) row are called.
  • Bottom Line — all five numbers in your ticket's third (bottom) row are called.
  • Full House — all 15 numbers on your ticket are called. This is the final prize and ends the game for everyone the moment it is claimed.

📢 How the Caller Works

Numbers are called automatically every 5 seconds by the host. You never need to draw numbers manually — the game handles it all.

  • The current called number is displayed large at the top of the screen
  • Recently called numbers are shown in a scrolling history below
  • Your ticket numbers are highlighted in orange the moment they are called
  • The CLAIM button appears automatically when you complete a prize pattern
  • The host can pause or resume the caller at any time

Strategy & Tips

🧠 Winning at Tambola

Tambola combines luck with alert attention. The ticket you receive is random, but how quickly you react to claim prizes is entirely up to you.

  • Watch the current number carefully. Numbers come every 5 seconds — it's easy to miss one if you're distracted. Keep your eyes on the caller display.
  • Claim the moment the button appears. The CLAIM button activates as soon as your pattern is complete. Another player may also be completing the same prize simultaneously — the first to tap wins.
  • Prioritise Full House if you're close. Full House ends the game. If you're one or two numbers away from a Full House and no other prizes remain, focus everything on getting there before others.
  • Don't overlook Early Five. Early Five is the easiest prize to win — it requires only 5 of your 15 numbers to be called. Claim it the instant the button lights up, even if other players seem close too.
  • Track which prizes are already taken. Once a prize is claimed, it is gone for everyone. Shift your focus to the remaining unclaimed prizes rather than watching for patterns that are already finished.

History & Origins

📖 About Tambola

Tambola (also called Housie) is the Indian variant of Bingo, enormously popular across South Asia. It is played at family gatherings, Diwali parties, corporate events, and school fetes across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and the wider diaspora.

Unlike Western Bingo which uses 5×5 cards with 25 numbers, Tambola uses a 3×9 ticket with only 15 numbers spread across 27 cells — making patterns harder to complete and the game slower and more social. Five distinct prizes are awarded rather than a single "Bingo" win, keeping players engaged throughout the full 90-number draw.

The name "Housie" comes from the British "Housey-Housey", the military slang for Bingo used by the British Army in India during the colonial era. "Tambola" became the more common name in South India and in formal or corporate settings, while "Housie" remains popular in North India and among older generations. Both names refer to exactly the same game with identical rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I miss a prize if I'm not paying attention?

Yes — prizes are first-come-first-claimed. If another player taps CLAIM for Early Five before you, that prize is gone even if your ticket also qualified. Stay alert and claim the moment the button appears.

What happens when Full House is claimed?

The game ends immediately for all players. All remaining unclaimed prizes are frozen — no further claims can be made. The Full House winner is announced and the game concludes.

How long does a game last?

A typical Tambola game runs approximately 8–12 minutes for a full 90-number draw at the default 5-second interval. Games end earlier if all five prizes are claimed before number 90 — Full House typically ends the game somewhere between numbers 55 and 80.

Can I play against friends?

Yes — Tambola supports 2–6 players. Share the room code with your friends and they can join from their own devices. The caller starts once the host begins the game.

How are tickets generated?

Tickets are generated randomly following official Tambola rules: exactly 5 numbers per row, 15 numbers per ticket in total, with each column restricted to its designated number range. No two players in the same game receive identical tickets.
